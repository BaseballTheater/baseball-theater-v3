**Gap:** The ratified scoreboard draws its date control as ‹ `SEP 21, 2026` › (`shell-scoreboard/DESIGN.md`; `web/src/components/scoreboard/DateControl.tsx`), which moves one day at a time. The owner wants a calendar (owner, 2026-11-07: *"I also want a calendar picker UI that makes it easy to switch the year, lets you pick with the calendar visible and the day of the week showing in the calendar grid, and the date display should let you type the date in yourself. If we could also show the number of games on each day in that view that would be great."*). Nothing ratified draws it, so S125 can't build it (rule 18).

**Impact:** Before S123, reaching a date long ago meant tapping back one day at a time. S123 impact: the owner approves how the scoreboard's calendar looks — typing a date, switching years, seeing the weekdays and each day's game count — before S125 builds it.

Future dates are pickable; nothing before 1901-04-18 is (owner, 2026-11-07, asked whether no-game days are dimmed and whether future dates are allowed: *"dimmed"*; *"allow future"*). The year control lists 1901 through next year (the agent's choice, for the owner to accept or change when ratifying: next year is the last year S124 answers for, after the owner's *"Reject past next year"* about that API, 2026-11-09). The amendment also says what a choice that lands before April 1901 does; what the calendar shows for a month after December of next year, including what the year control reads then (a typed date can still reach one, and S124 answers 400 `month_too_late` for it); and what a month shows when S136's month function answers unknown: no day with games and at least one day BT holds no schedule for (every count `null` for December 2019, which S122's backfill skips; it stays unknown until every one of its days has been stored), since it otherwise looks like an off-season month. The amendment also draws or states each of these, from the owner's answers of 2026-11-10 (quoted in the note below):
    - **Day cells.** Each day's number is bold, and the smaller game count under it is drawn at 50% opacity (owner, 2026-11-10: *"The smaller numbers underneath the calendar dates should be 50% opaque and the date numbers should be bolded"*).
    - **Container.** On mobile the calendar is a sheet that slides up from the bottom over a dimmed page; on medium and desktop it is a dropdown under the date control. On mobile it is always that sheet, never a full-screen dialog, with no drag handle and no dragging.
    - **Closing.** No `Close` button at any size. Mobile gets a small ✕; medium and desktop get none. A click or tap outside the calendar closes it at every size, and so does Esc, which closes it without changing the date (owner, 2026-11-10: *"Esc closes the calendar view without changing the date."*). On mobile, opening or closing the calendar never changes the URL or adds to the browser's history (owner, 2026-11-10: *"the calendar shouldn't change the URL or back stack on mobile."*), so a phone's Back button or gesture while the sheet is open does what it does with the calendar closed (the agent's reading of that answer, which the owner confirms when the redrawn draft is shown).
    - **Today.** The `Today` button stays. While the calendar shows today's month it picks today and closes the calendar; while it shows any other month it moves the calendar to today's month and leaves it open. The amendment says which day "today" is when the season is over (the draft used the day the scoreboard opens on).
    - **Month arrows.** ‹ and › in the calendar's header move it one month. On a January ‹ and on a December › cross into the neighbouring year and the year control follows (owner, 2026-11-10, asked whether they do: *"yes, they do."*). The amendment says what they do at the two ends, April 1901 and December of next year. The one-day ‹ › arrows beside the date control stay.
    - **Speed.** The calendar opens already filled: the month it opens on shows its counts in the first frame, with no loading state, no fade and no reflow, and the amendment gives the slide's duration. The "month still loading its counts" state is drawn only for a month the fan moves to.
    - **Height.** Every month reserves six week rows, so the calendar never changes height between months. A short mobile screen is drawn with the game counts dropped, and the amendment gives the screen height below which they go; that is decided once from the screen, never per month. The grid scrolls inside the sheet only when even that doesn't fit.
