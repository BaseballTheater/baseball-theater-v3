**Gap:** Once the World Series is over, the owner wants Standings to say who won it, in a celebratory box above the regular season's table (owner, 2026-11-10: *"A week after the last playoff game, we should switch back to the regular season by default for the standings, but with a box above that says like blank team won the World Series, view the playoffs standings page or whatever"*). Nothing BT serves names the winner: the standings answer holds the regular season's records, and the World Series schedule is read only for the season's last game day (`mapSeasonCalendar`, `functions/src/mappers/season-calendar.ts`).

**Impact:** Before S140, BT's data never said who won the World Series. S140 impact: in the off-season the standings data names the champion, so S130's page can show the owner's celebratory box. Nothing on the page changes yet.

**Acceptance criteria** (checks `scripts/verify-S140.sh` performs)

1. A season's calendar says who won its World Series, read from the World Series schedule the calendar already asks MLB for (`worldSeriesUrl`; no new request). One named, tested function decides it: the winner is the team whose series wins, in the season's last final World Series game that wasn't cancelled, are more than half of that game's `gamesInSeries` (4 of 7; 5 of 9 in 1903 and 1919–1921). It answers no winner while the series is under way, for a season with no World Series (1901, 1902, 1904, 1994), for an empty schedule, and when the payload lacks `gamesInSeries` or the teams' series records: never a guess (tests on `fixtures/raw/worldseries-2025.json`: the Dodgers, in seven games; `worldseries-2026.json`: the Brewers, in five; `worldseries-2027.json`, which is empty: none; the 2026 recording cut to its first four games: none; a payload with neither field: none).
2. The answer of `GET /api/v1/standings` gains `champion`: `{ season, teamId, name, wonOn }` on a day in a season's off-season (the days `standingsPhase` picks that season's `final` table) that is on or after `wonOn`, once that season has a winner by AC 1, and `null` on every other day. The off-season starts the day after the regular season ends, while the playoffs are still being played, and MLB's schedule for a past season already holds the deciding game, so a day before it names no champion: a past date gets the answer it would have had on that day (as S139 AC 1 says of the views). `wonOn` is the day of the game that decided the Series, the game AC 1 reads the winner from, so the page can count days from it (S130 AC 4). It is the same for every `view`, and for a past date it is that date's season's (`?date=2025-11-15`: the Dodgers, 2025, `wonOn` 2025-11-01; `?date=2026-11-10`: the Brewers, 2026, `wonOn` 2026-10-28; `?date=2026-10-28`, the day the Series was won: the Brewers; `?date=2026-10-27` and `?date=2026-10-01`, off-season days before it: `null`; `?date=2026-09-05` and a spring day: `null`) (route tests on the recordings).
3. MLB request budget is unchanged: a standings request asks MLB nothing more than it does before this story (test with the counting client).
4. The OpenAPI spec carries the new property (`pnpm api:spec`), `pnpm api:breaking` exits 0 (additive), and the web client's generated types are rendered from it again (`web/src/api/generated/`).

**Notes:**

- **Where the winner is.** Each game in MLB's World Series schedule carries `gamesInSeries`, and each side its record in the series so far (`teams.away.leagueRecord.wins`, and the same for home) with `isWinner` for that game. In `worldseries-2026.json` the last game (2026-10-28) has the Brewers on 4 wins of a 7-game series. `mapSeasonCalendar` already walks these games for `lastGameDate` and skips cancelled ones; reuse that walk.
- **When the box shows is the design's to say** (S129): the owner's words put it above the regular season's table from a week after the last playoff game. This story serves the winner from the day the Series was won (`wonOn`) to the off-season's end, and S130's domain function applies S129's rule to it.
- **2026-11-10 backlog review: `wonOn` added.** This note said the page could tell the week had passed from S139's `views.default`. That only holds when a team is marked as clinched: for a season whose table marks none (S139's note allows for them) `playoffs` is never offered, the default is the regular season from the first day after the Series, and the box would show a week early. And if the owner's answer to how long the box stays is anything other than "until spring training's first game" (asked, not yet answered), the page needs the date to count from. So the answer carries it; it is read from the same game as the winner, with no new request.
- **AC 2's `null` from spring's first game rides on that same unanswered question.** If the owner wants the box to stay past the start of spring training, AC 2's days change before this story ships (rule 7), not after.
- **Team name only.** The schedule names each team and gives its ID, not its abbreviation; the page already draws a team from its ID (`TeamSwatch`).
- `web/src/api/generated.types.test.ts` lists each response type and `pnpm verify`'s drift gate (`web/src/api/codegen.test.ts`) re-renders `web/src/api/generated/` from the spec.

- **Still asked of the owner (2026-11-10, evening):** how long the box stays. The owner asked whether the question was about the link to the playoffs after they end; the coordinator said yes and proposed that both the playoffs choice (S139 AC 1) and this box go when spring training starts. AC 2's days change before this story ships if the answer differs (rule 7).
